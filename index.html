How does SAP IBP Demand Sensing actually detect the ARM & HAMMER demand spike? ▼
SAP IBP Demand Sensing uses a Hybrid Gradient Boosting (xGBoost) algorithm. It ingests daily POS data from SAP Customer Activity Repository, along with sales orders (S/4HANA SD), promotions (TPM), and inventory (MM). It creates daily-granularity forecasts for a 4–8 week horizon and automatically detects outliers in sales history. In this demo scenario, POS velocity on ARM & HAMMER Baking Soda showed a +24.8% spike — the outlier correction algorithm validated this as a genuine sustained uplift (not a data quality artifact). Source: SAP Help Portal, Demand Sensing Process documentation.
What did you remove from v5 and why? ▼
Three major removals: (1) TikTok social signal — replaced with SAP IBP/CAR-native POS anomaly because social listening is not a SAP capability and requires external API integration. (2) "Ciner Group mine flood" — a fictional event using a real company name, creating defamation risk. Replaced with generic "Tier-1 supplier force majeure" via SAP Ariba Risk Management. (3) "Scarcity index 94/100" — a fictional metric; SAP Business Network does not publish a public scarcity index. Replaced with the real BN capability: component planning collaboration constraint signals. Also corrected all benchmark KPIs to reflect published CPG industry data (OEE, fill rate, MAPE, trade ROI).
How is the ILWU reroute scenario supported? ▼
The ILWU (International Longshore and Warehouse Union) is a real union with historically recurring West Coast port strike actions (last major action 2023). Container tracking via SAP Business Network for Logistics was released as generally available in Q3 2024 (confirmed in SAP BN release notes). The reroute cost estimate ($28k premium vs $180k potential downtime) is illustrative — the $28k premium for East Coast port rerouting is directionally plausible but not validated. The core scenario structure is SAP-supportable and the risk is real.
